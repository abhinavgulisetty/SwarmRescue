# Showing the simulation clearly

Use the focused cases first. A fixed camera and one robot make the causal event
visible; the full swarm mission is easier to explain afterwards.

Generate the complete player, clips, storyboards and measured event traces:

```bash
.venv/bin/python -m swarmrescue.cli cases --out outputs/cases
```

Open `outputs/cases/index.html`. It works locally without a server or internet
connection. Choose a situation, play it, or select an event button to jump to
that point. The event times are simulated seconds; the player converts them to
video time. Download links provide the original MP4 files for slides.

| Case | What to point out | What to say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **01: Find a survivor** | The wall blocks the red sight line. After the robot goes around, a local reading becomes possible. Contact is confirmed only within 0.7 m. | “We can see the survivor in this explanation view. The robot has to get a visible local reading and physically approach before it confirms contact.” |
| **02: Passage blocked** | The member starts suspended, falls after its support is released, and blocks the original route. The old path stays red; the new path is green. Blue shows where the robot actually drove. | “The old route becomes unusable. After scanning the fallen geometry, the integer planner calculates a detour and the wheel controller drives it.” |
| **03: Escort outside** | Contact starts the escort. The survivor follows the driven trail. The robot pauses when the gap grows. The green outdoor boundary and safe zone define completion. | “Reaching someone does not finish a rescue. The survivor must physically get outside. Watch the robot wait and then guide them across the boundary.” |
| **04: No usable route** | Wider debris closes every passage. No replacement route is returned; the robot remains on the near side. | “Sometimes there is no route. The correct response here is to stop and report that, rather than drive through a blocked path.” |

At the checked default settings, clip lengths are approximately 11, 28, 11 and
5 seconds. The player provides chapters for contact, falling debris, replanning,
waiting, evacuation and the no-route result. Storyboards show the initial state,
the key event and the final state. JSON files include timestamps, measured
positions, routes and sampled trajectories.

To generate one case or change playback speed:

```bash
.venv/bin/python -m swarmrescue.cli cases --case reroute --speed 1 --out outputs/reroute
.venv/bin/python -m swarmrescue.cli cases --no-render --out outputs/case-checks
```

The clips use small authored teaching layouts. The search site is supplied; case
01 explains local detection/contact, not cooperative beacon localization. Debris
release is timed, not generated by a thermal damage model. Routing receives the
complete current structural map and uses the native integer kernel. These cases
do not claim distributed inference, radio performance, or ARM execution. Those
components are exercised in the separate full mission.

Robots move through wheel actuators. Survivors are upright force-driven capsule
proxies with ideal balance and low sliding resistance, as in the full rescue
simulation. Their positions are not overwritten during the run. Evacuation
requires a contacted survivor's physical position to cross at least 0.5 m beyond
the building footprint at supported ground height. The escort case uses a stable
pause threshold: wait at 0.75 m gap and resume after it closes to 0.55 m.

Generated clips live under `outputs/` and are not committed. The source, player
template, instructions and physical acceptance tests are committed, so a fresh
checkout can regenerate the demonstrations with the command above.

To reproduce the full mission from source after the focused cases:

```bash
.venv/bin/python -m swarmrescue.cli rescue --render --height 778 \
  --fps 12 --speed 2 --out outputs/cases/full-mission \
  --report outputs/cases/full-mission.json
```
